Photo cloud storage and video cloud storage

Every photo and video you take is copied to your account automatically, in the background, without you opening the app or remembering to do anything. This is the feature most people install a cloud backup app for, and the one that matters on the day a phone is lost, stolen or dropped in water.

As a file backup app it treats photo cloud storage and video cloud storage the same way: originals in, originals out, at full quality rather than shrunken copies. That applies equally to online photo storage and to online video storage, where a resized copy is the thing you cannot undo later.

The part worth getting right at setup is folder selection. Most backup tools cover only your camera folder, which captures photos you took yourself and misses everything else. Screenshots holding booking confirmations, PDFs in your downloads, and photos family sent through messaging apps all live elsewhere.

Backups run on a schedule that suits the phone rather than interrupting it, typically while charging, idle or on Wi-Fi. The first upload is the large one because it carries your whole history, and everything after that is just the handful of files you created that day. If you have ever avoided a backup app because you assumed it would flatten your battery or eat a data allowance, this is the part that answers it.

  • Choose any folder, including screenshots, downloads and messaging media
  • Wi-Fi only by default, with mobile data optional

One-tap restore to any new phone

A backup is only as good as the restore. One-tap restore brings your entire library onto a new or freshly reset device without selecting files individually or sitting through a manual download queue.

Because your library lives in online file storage rather than a manufacturer backup, moving between Android and iPhone is a sign-in rather than a migration project.

This matters most on the day you change phones, which is also the day people most commonly lose things. Confirm your final backup completed before wiping the old device, because once a factory reset finishes there is no recovering what was never uploaded.

Restoring is also useful outside of upgrades. If a phone is repaired, replaced under warranty, or reset to fix a software problem, the same process puts your library back without you rebuilding anything by hand. Because the files live in your account rather than in a device backup tied to one manufacturer, you are never stuck choosing a new phone based on what your old backup will talk to.

Vault Lock: secure cloud storage for private documents

Backing up protects you from losing your phone. It does nothing about someone picking your phone up and scrolling through it. Those are different problems, and Vault Lock solves the second one.

If secure cloud storage is what you are after, this is the part that matters day to day. Encryption protects files while they move across a network. A passcode layer protects them from a person holding an unlocked device.

Adding a passcode layer to specific folders keeps sensitive documents closed even on an unlocked device. The documents worth protecting are usually identification scans, tax paperwork, insurance records and anything showing bank details.

Keep the vault narrow. People who lock everything tend to find the extra step irritating and switch it off within a fortnight, which leaves them less protected than a small vault they actually keep using.

It is worth being clear about what a vault does and does not cover. Encryption in transit protects your files while they move between your phone and CloudGate. A passcode layer protects them from a person holding your unlocked device. Both matter, they solve different problems, and only using one of them leaves an obvious gap.

  • Separate passcode layer, independent of your phone lock
  • Applies to folders you choose rather than everything
  • Contents stay hidden from the main file browser
  • Still backed up and restorable like any other file

How it works

How a cloud backup app actually works.

The plain version of what the app does on your behalf.

When you enable automatic backup, CloudGate is given permission to read the folders you selected. From that point it watches those folders for anything new. When a photo, video or document appears, it is queued for upload and sent to your account the next time the conditions you set are met, which usually means the phone is on Wi-Fi and not in the middle of something demanding.

Your original file never moves. What travels is a copy, and the copy is what lands in your account. This is why backing up does not free space by itself, and why people sometimes assume nothing happened: from the phone's point of view, nothing visibly changed.

Once a file is uploaded it stops being tied to the device it came from. You can open it in the web app on a computer that has never seen your phone, restore it onto a completely different handset, or share it with someone who has no idea what CloudGate is. That separation between your files and any one piece of hardware is the entire value of cloud backup, and it is the reason a lost phone stops being a catastrophe and becomes an inconvenience.

The uploads themselves are encrypted in transit, so the connection carrying your files is protected while data moves across it. Files are then stored against your account, reachable only by you unless you deliberately share something.

Storage

What 100GB free cloud storage actually holds.

What 100GB means in photos, video and documents on a modern phone.

25k–40kphotos fit in 100GB
60–100MBper minute of 1080p video
350MB+per minute of 4K video

A single photo from a current phone camera usually lands between 2MB and 5MB, depending on detail and lighting. That puts one gigabyte at roughly 250 to 400 photos, and 100GB somewhere between 25,000 and 40,000 images. Portrait mode shots and RAW files run larger, but for everyday photography those figures hold up.

Video is the item that changes the maths. A minute of 1080p footage costs around 60MB to 100MB. The same minute recorded in 4K can reach 350MB or more, which means ten minutes of 4K uses about as much room as a thousand photographs. If your library looks modest but your storage is disappearing, video is almost always the reason.

Documents are effectively free by comparison. A scanned receipt is a few hundred kilobytes and a long PDF perhaps two or three megabytes. You could scan every piece of paper in your house and barely register it against 100GB.

The practical takeaway: if you mostly shoot photos with occasional short clips, 100GB will hold years of material comfortably. If you record long 4K video regularly, expect it to fill faster and plan on archiving finished footage rather than keeping everything live indefinitely.

Worth knowing

Is this the best cloud storage for you?

A tool is easier to trust when its limits are stated plainly rather than discovered later.

CloudGate is personal cloud storage in the literal sense. Photos, videos, documents, contacts and the paperwork that accumulates around ordinary life. It is aimed at people who want that material protected without becoming interested in how backup works, which is most people.

If you have been comparing a free cloud storage app against paid online file storage, the honest question is not which has the longest feature list. It is which one you will still have running in six months.

It is not a collaboration suite. If you need several people editing the same document simultaneously, shared team drives with permission tiers, or version history across a working file, those are different products solving a different problem and you should use one of them instead.

It also does not free up phone storage on its own. Backing up creates a second copy and leaves your originals in place, so reclaiming local space still means deleting local copies once you have confirmed the backup holds them. That is deliberate, because software that deletes your files automatically is software that will eventually delete something you wanted.

And it cannot recover anything that was deleted before it was ever backed up. If a photo left your phone last year and no backup existed at the time, no service can retrieve it. Protection starts the day you switch it on, which is the strongest argument for not leaving the setup for another weekend.

  • No lock-in, since you can download your files at any time
