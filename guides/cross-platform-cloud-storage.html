Cross platform cloud storage across Android, iOS and web

One account, every device. How multi device cloud storage removes the emailing files to yourself habit, and why it makes switching phone platforms a sign-in rather than a migration.

12 min read Updated {{ page.last_modified_at | date: "%-d %B %Y" }} Android, iPhone and web Beginner friendly

Most people's files are not in one place. They are scattered across a phone, a laptop, an email account used as an improvised filing cabinet, and a messaging thread with themselves. Finding anything means remembering which device you were holding at the time.

Cross platform cloud storage fixes that by making the device irrelevant. One account holds the files, and Android, iPhone and a browser are simply three windows onto the same thing. Add something from one and it is available from the others.

This guide covers what changes when you stop organising files by device, how to access files across devices in practice, what happens when you switch between Android and iPhone, and the household case for sharing one allowance rather than several.

One account, seen from different screens

This is the whole idea, and it is worth stating plainly because the mental shift matters more than any setting.

Your files stop belonging to a device. They belong to an account, and the device is just how you happen to be looking at them today. Sign in on an Android phone, an iPhone and a laptop browser at the same time and all three show the same library.

That sounds obvious written down, and it changes daily habits more than people expect. You stop thinking "that photo is on my old phone" and start thinking "that photo is in my account". The question of which device holds the original disappears, because there is no original in the device sense any more.

The practical consequences show up in small moments:

  • Photograph a document on your phone and attach it from your laptop ten seconds later, with no transfer step.
  • Save a file on a work computer and read it on the train without having sent it anywhere.
  • Hand someone a photo from whichever device is nearest, rather than the one that took it.
  • Lose a phone and lose only the hardware.

Cloud storage for Android iOS and web from a single account is what multi device cloud storage should mean in practice, rather than three separate free accounts you have to remember the boundaries of.

Mobile and web cloud storage running from the same place is what makes this work. Cloud storage across devices is not three services kept in step with each other, it is one library that several devices happen to be looking at.

The habit this replaces

Almost everyone has emailed a file to themselves. It is the universal workaround for moving something between two devices, and it half works, which is why it survives.

What it actually costs:

  • Attachment size limits. Anything large, particularly video, simply will not send.
  • Version confusion. Three emails with similar attachments and no reliable way to tell which is current.
  • An inbox turning into storage. Searching for a file competes with every other message you have ever received.
  • No structure. Files arrive in chronological order regardless of what they are.

Once a shared account exists, this stops being necessary. Access files anywhere becomes the default rather than something you arrange in advance, and the inbox goes back to being for messages.

A useful test of whether this is set up properly: the next time you want a file on another device, notice whether you reach for email. If you do, something is not signed in where it should be.

How to access files across devices in practice

The setup is short. Most of it is signing in.

  • Install the app on every phone or tablet you use and sign in with the same account on each.
  • Turn on automatic backup on the phone you take photos with, which is usually just one device.
  • Bookmark the web app on any computer you use regularly, rather than installing anything.
  • Check the same recent file appears everywhere before you rely on it.

That last step is the one worth not skipping. Seeing the same photo on a phone and in a browser confirms the account is genuinely shared rather than two accounts that look similar.

What each device is best at

They are not equivalent, and knowing which to reach for saves time.

The phone app is the only thing that captures photos automatically, because it can watch your camera folder in the background. It is also the most convenient for grabbing something quickly.

The browser is better for anything in bulk: uploading a folder from a computer, downloading a batch of photos for editing, or hunting through a large library on a big screen.

A second phone or tablet mostly reads rather than writes. Worth signing in anyway, since it costs nothing and means the files are there when that is the device in your hand.

Switching between Android and iPhone

This is where cloud storage Android and iPhone side by side stops being a convenience and becomes the reason people can change their minds about phones at all.

Manufacturer device backups are tied to their platform. An Android backup expects to restore onto Android, and an iPhone backup onto an iPhone. That is fine until you want to move the other way, at which point people discover their entire photo library is locked inside a backup that only speaks one language.

Files in a cross platform account have no such problem. They are not in a device backup, they are in an account, and an account does not care what operating system is asking.

So moving from Android to iPhone, or back, becomes:

  • Confirm the old phone finished backing up and open a couple of files from your account to check.
  • Sign in on the new phone with the same account.
  • Turn automatic backup on on the new device.

That is the migration. Your library is available immediately rather than needing to be transferred, because it was never on the old handset alone.

Cloud storage covers your files. Contacts, messages and app data move separately through each platform's own tools, so check those before wiping anything. The new phone guide covers the full order of operations.

The wider point is that share storage between devices in this sense removes a constraint from buying decisions. If you have ever kept a phone partly because changing platforms sounded like a data problem, that is the constraint going away.

Access photos across devices without duplicating them

A common worry is that signing in everywhere will create copies of everything on every device, filling them all up. It does not work that way.

The files live in your account. A device shows you what is there and downloads individual items when you open them. Browsing your library on a tablet does not pull twenty thousand photos onto the tablet.

Which means the sensible arrangement is usually:

  • One phone doing automatic backup, the one you actually take photos with.
  • Every other device signed in but not backing up, so they can read without competing.
  • The browser on any computer for bulk work.

If two phones both take photos, back up from both. The account handles them arriving from different places without any special configuration.

This is also what makes it possible to access files from phone and computer simultaneously rather than switching between them. Both are looking at the same library at the same time, and neither is holding the only copy.

Common problems and how to fix them

My files show on one device but not another. Almost always two different accounts rather than a sync failure. Check the email address signed in on each, since people often create a second account without noticing.

Something I added on my laptop has not appeared on my phone. Usually the upload had not finished when the tab closed, or the phone has not refreshed. Reopen the app and pull to refresh.

My new phone is not showing my old photos. Check you signed into the same account rather than creating a new one during setup, which is easy to do when a sign-up screen appears mid-flow.

Both my phones are backing up and my storage is filling fast. That is expected with two devices uploading. Decide whether both need automatic backup or only the one you photograph with.

I switched platforms and my messages did not come across. Cloud storage covers files, not messages or contacts. Those move through each platform's own transfer tools and need handling separately.

My tablet is downloading everything and filling up. Check whether backup was switched on there by accident. A secondary device should generally read rather than upload.

The whole thing as a checklist

  • Use one account rather than several free ones on different devices
  • Sign in on every phone, tablet and browser you use regularly
  • Turn on automatic backup only on the phone you photograph with
  • Confirm the same recent file appears on two different devices
  • Bookmark the web app rather than installing on shared computers
  • Before changing phone platforms, check contacts and messages separately
  • For a household, compare a shared plan per person against separate accounts

The check that matters is the fourth one. Two devices showing the same file is proof the account is genuinely shared, and it is the assumption everything else here rests on.
